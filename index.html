Food trends 2027 by audience

For CPG innovation teams

Matcha extensions and texture formats justify NPD investment now. The base matcha ingredient is at 5.6 percent menu share and still growing 26.1 percent, so the window for a differentiated format is open rather than crowded. Cold foam, up 57 percent, is the cheapest texture claim to test.

For foodservice operators

Attachment is where the 2027 growth sits. Wings at 10.6 percent menu share and fries at 12.2 percent are already on your line, so new sauces and topping systems raise check without new inventory. Tacos carry a new flavor with no format education required.

For retailers and category managers

Shelf allocation should follow specialty beverages and occasion bundles. The event need grew 48.7 percent, which supports named occasion merchandising over generic seasonal resets. Space taken from declining specialty flavors funds the move.

What are the top food trends for 2027?

The top food trends for 2027 are matcha, growing 26.1 percent year over year. Tacos, up 17.4 percent, carry global flavors in a handheld format. Chicken wings, up 16.7 percent, drive sauce innovation. Steak, up 16.3 percent, leads protein premiumization. Loaded fries, up 10.8 percent, add topping systems. What is the biggest food trend for 2027?

Matcha is the biggest food trend for 2027. Matcha grew 26.1 percent year over year in US social food conversation as of July 2026 and holds a 5.6 percent US menu share, making it the fastest growing ingredient at meaningful scale. Lifecycle stage places a trend in one of four positions, emerging, trending, mature or declining. A trend qualifies as a 2027 call when growth and lifecycle stage agree, based on US social, menu and retail food data, July 2026. The full model sits in the 2027 food trends forecast by category.

What is the difference between a food trend and a fad?

A food trend is a sustained shift in demand that shows growth across several independent signals at once, typically social conversation, menu penetration and retail presence. A fad is a single channel spike, usually social only, that never converts into menu share or repeat purchase. The practical test is durability across signals, not the size of the initial spike.
